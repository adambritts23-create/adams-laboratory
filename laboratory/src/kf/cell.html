Chemical equations

B denotes the reagent base (RN in your figure); ROH denotes the alcohol. Ionic notation below makes the charge balance explicit.

Generator electrode

(1) Anode (+), oxidation: 2 I⁻ → I₂ + 2 e⁻

(2) Cathode (−), reduction: 2 BH⁺ + 2 e⁻ → 2 B + H₂(g)

Formation of alkyl sulfite

(3) ROH + SO₂ + B ⇌ BH⁺ + ROSO₂⁻

Alcohol-rich reagent favours alkyl-sulfite formation. Methanol corresponds to R = CH₃.

Reaction in the reagent and overall KF reaction

(4) Reduction: I₂ + 2 e⁻ → 2 I⁻

(5) Oxidation: ROSO₂⁻ + H₂O + 2 B → ROSO₃⁻ + 2 BH⁺ + 2 e⁻

(6) Combined: ROSO₂⁻ + H₂O + I₂ + 2 B → ROSO₃⁻ + 2 BH⁺ + 2 I⁻

(7) Overall KF: ROH + SO₂ + 3 B + I₂ + H₂O → ROSO₃⁻ + 3 BH⁺ + 2 I⁻

These are the reactions in your diagram written with dissociated ions. The two solution half-reactions are electron bookkeeping, not electron paths through the liquid. Cathodic chemistry is reagent-dependent; the displayed hydrogen reaction follows your schematic.
