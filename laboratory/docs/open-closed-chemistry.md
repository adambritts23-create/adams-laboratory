# Open general closed chemistry — review report

This phase expands the existing conditional aqueous closed-equilibrium path. It does not establish an arbitrary-reagent, multiphase or gas-complete solver. The reviewed Step-5 experiment remains unchanged and separately selectable. No deployment or push was performed.

1. **Why the earlier experience was chemistry-specific.** Seven imported component identities had reviewed conservation seeds. Exact Fe/peroxide component selection automatically selected a restricted experiment, its fixed preparation and its addition sweep. A 32-species resource ceiling rejected the full chloride network. Neutral physical reagents were not represented separately from ionic components. These were distinct metadata, routing, preparation and capacity restrictions; numerical convergence alone cannot remove them.

2. **Reviewed-profile routing.** Normal Closed reagent / automatic redox now always audits the general source graph and offers editable supplied amounts. Selecting Fe²⁺, H₂O₂, H⁺, Cl⁻ and water no longer chooses Step 5. The explicitly loaded example under Validated examples / experiments retains the original 24 admitted reactions, original exclusions, exact recipe, sweep and results. The same component selection in general mode admits 32 reactions, including chloride redox. These are different thermodynamic scopes and can give different equilibria; the general result does not inherit the Step-5 validation claim. Returning from general mode restores ordinary settings, including when entered from the reviewed example.

3. **Component metadata coverage.** All 168 imported components were audited; none has a structured elemental-composition field. Their source element associations are discovery links, not authoritative atom counts. `componentMetadata.js` adds a separate versioned identity registry, retaining the original seven seeds and adding Eu³⁺, K⁺, CrO₄²⁻, Cr³⁺ and Cr²⁺. Coverage is now 12 fully allocated component identities; 156 have partial discovery information and still require composition/charge review. Every missing case and reason is in `open-closed-metadata-coverage.json`. SUPPORTED in that table means metadata allocation, not permission to solve every equilibrium containing that component.

   The additional seeds bind exact imported IDs, component SHA-256 and database provenance. Europium curation uses the imported 85Bar/Par Eu(III)/Eu(II) source record and the explicit [europium(III) chloride identity](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24809). Potassium/chromate composition is supported by the explicit [potassium chromate identity](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/24597). Cr²⁺/Cr³⁺ are explicitly curated monatomic chromous/chromic source identities. No runtime formula parsing or external lookup occurs. No new oxidation-state allocation is guessed: unallocated oxidation-state inventory remains unresolved in inspection. Complete repository integrity remains an independent prerequisite; the old Step-5 registry is untouched.

4. **Physical reagent contract.** `reagentPreparation.js` distinguishes component identities from neutral reagent recipes. Its small catalogue contains FeCl₂, HCl, H₂O₂ and EuCl₃ solutions. Each recipe records identity/formula, component coefficients, counterions, elemental composition and provenance. Recipe composition and charge are checked against the source-bound registry. `prepareReagent` accepts molality and solvent mass in kg; its output records moles and mol/kg H₂O. `mixPreparations` sums actual solute moles and solvent masses before calculating mixture molalities. Both use immutable branded preparations; copied objects are refused. Molarity, volume and hydration/density assumptions are not silently converted. A future volume-based stock contract must supply density, solvent content and a reviewed conversion. No large reagent catalogue was added.

5. **Electroneutrality UX.** Bare components retain strict explicit countercharge. The diagnostic reports each supplied component, its charge equivalents, net charge and required opposite charge. For 0.001 mol/kg Fe²⁺ it reports +0.002 mol charge equivalents/kg and requires −0.002. Examples suggest a compatible explicit counterion or an identified physical reagent without choosing one. The arithmetic allowance is exactly the pre-existing closed-preparation allowance. The UI also offers explicit recipe doses per kg of current solvent; FeCl₂ adds one Fe and two Cl inventories. All component forms must already be selected; missing forms are explained rather than silently inserted.

6. **General conservation.** The existing source-equation propagation, elemental/charge transport, elimination of formal electron, H−2O solvent convention, physical-basis rank checks and simultaneous mass-action solve are reused. The Eu family uses the same path as Fe; chromate exercises a polyatomic seed. Source halves retain unequal electron coefficients and proton/water coupling. All compatible aqueous hydrolysis, complexation and counterion-redox rows remain. New allocation metadata do not determine an equilibrium answer. A real source-label collision, `EuCl 2+` versus `EuCl2+`, was found: the existing preparation boundary strips spaces for duplicate detection. Only colliding internal display names now receive their source-ID suffix. Numerical rows and IDs are unchanged, and inspection retains exact source formulas. No solver equations or tolerances changed.

7. **Compiler capacity.** Inspection found explicit size guards, not mathematics requiring 32 or a condition-number test tied to 32. In the absence of Git history, the original historical rationale cannot be established. The limit functions as an implementation/resource bound. An isolated `.local/open-closed-capacity` probe widened guards only and tested real networks of 9, 29, 36, 47 and 55 source species. The 36-species full Fe/chloride/peroxide and 47-species Fe/Eu networks solved and passed existing acceptance checks. The 55-species Fe/Cr network converged aqueously but was correctly refused by the phase audit. Warm runs were approximately 0.13–0.25 seconds in the initial probe; timing is machine-dependent, not a performance guarantee.

   Production closed compilation now permits at most 64 source species and 64 source reactions, retaining 16 basis coordinates and all existing rank, cycle consistency, bookkeeping and numerical acceptance checks. Shared reaction-basis transformation remains at 32 by default; closed compilation explicitly opts into `closed-aqueous-64-v1`. Ordinary/Pourbaix callers are not widened. Tests refuse 65 species, unknown capacity policies, rank-deficient enlarged networks and contradictory cycles. This is a bounded resource increase, not proof that all networks below the ceiling are well conditioned. No tolerance was relaxed to accept a larger calculation.

8. **Graph reduction findings.** Structurally unreachable source rows are already omitted by reachability. Exact inverse/cycle-equivalent rows are checked by the basis algebra; they are not discarded before source-consistency validation. Candidates requiring unknown component identities remain unavailable. No concentration-based pruning, answer-driven selection or new phase suppression was introduced. Phase irrelevance is checked after solving the declared aqueous candidate. Removing a row solely because its element total is zero would additionally require proving there is no signed-inventory/reservoir path; that optimization was not introduced. Redundant reaction extents can lack a unique source decomposition: the Fe/Eu control reports that explanation unavailable while retaining a validated equilibrium. This does not invent a unique kinetic pathway.

9. **Solids integration readiness.** General closed chemistry still diagnoses solids rather than solving their inventories. The existing ordinary assemblage enumerator can eventually be reused, but it is not a drop-in switch. `closedRedoxNetwork.js` admits aqueous physical carriers only; the closed preparation/acceptance/inspection code currently assumes aqueous amounts, constructs dissolved elemental totals, validates solvent exchange from those amounts and publishes an empty accepted-solids list. A proper integration must add solid conservation weights, unit solid activities, complementarity/assemblage checks, solid contributions to H−2O and charge, result branding, and selected-result partition adapters together. Enabling a solid flag alone would produce incomplete accounting. A bounded precipitation benchmark was therefore not enabled. The Fe/Cr refusal below is a concrete future benchmark candidate, not an accepted precipitation result.

10. **Gas/headspace roadmap.** Dissolved H₂/O₂/O₃ are aqueous carriers. A gas candidate currently supplies a hypothetical normalized fugacity; it has no accepted gas inventory. A fixed-fugacity reservoir is an open boundary and cannot be substituted for finite headspace. A minimal future finite-headspace contract needs gas identities/source laws, temperature, headspace volume, solvent mass, initial gas moles and declared ideal-gas assumptions. It must couple dissolved–gas equilibrium with gas elemental/reducing inventories and `n = pV/(RT)`, using consistent standard states. Open atmosphere would instead fix selected fugacities and explicitly cease conserving their exchanged inventories. Benchmark a single nonreactive gas partition first, then a redox gas with independent mole/pressure and potential checks. No gas kinetics, bubbles, atmosphere or headspace implementation was added. Current phase/gas exclusions remain visible; low hypothetical fugacity still means CONDITIONAL, not proof that headspace is irrelevant.

11. **Additional metal validation.** The Eu control supplies Eu³⁺ = 1e−6, H⁺ = 0.01 and Cl⁻ = 0.010003 mol/kg H₂O. General discovery admits 24 source equations and 27 aqueous carriers. No Eu/peroxide pair scope exists. The independent script imports only the raw database: it substitutes raw formation laws onto Eu³⁺/Cl⁻/H⁺/e⁻ coordinates, then solves the four analytical sums with a separate analytic-Jacobian Newton calculation. It does not import production discovery, the basis compiler or the equilibrium solver. Production gives pH 2.000864206621187 and Eh 0.7496764283124613 V vs SHE. Independent pH error is 4.44e−16, pe error 5.33e−15, and maximum carrier log-ratio difference 5.69e−14. Every carrier, not just total Eu, is compared. Eu(II) is a trace in this oxidizing control; this is not a claim of large Eu electron transfer or independent validation of an entire concentration domain. The mixed Fe/Eu control is additional internal integration evidence only.

12. **Fe/dichromate readiness.** Cr metadata, Cr(VI)/Cr(III) source connectivity, chromate protonation/dimerization, Cr(III) hydrolysis, Fe–chromate complexation, potassium/counterion rows, candidate phases and network capacity are now available for this target audit. Source record `spana:2ac52a30213c9288:94892` expresses dichromate as 2 H⁺ + 2 chromate − water, log K 14.54. Two Cr(VI)→Cr(III) changes require six electrons; the retained source half-laws have three-electron coefficients and exact common-multiple cancellation. No six-electron constant was invented.

   The audit represents 6e−6 FeCl₂ + 1e−6 K₂Cr₂O₇ + 0.01 HCl through equivalent source inventories: Fe²⁺ 6e−6, chromate 2e−6, K⁺ 2e−6, H⁺ 0.010002, Cl⁻ 0.010012. The water difference is handled by the existing unit-solvent H−2O convention, not an added finite-water amount. This source-coordinate transformation is explicitly documented for research and is not a new user-selectable dichromate recipe. The 55-species/51-reaction graph solves an aqueous candidate, but Fe₂O₃(cr) log saturation ratio is about 0.97377 and FeOOH(cr) about 0.26639, above the unchanged acceptance threshold. The candidate is withheld. Remaining blockers: accepted closed-solid inventories, a reviewed dichromate stock/recipe mapping, independent multiphase benchmark and domain validation, and any required finite headspace for other conditions. No trusted arbitrary Fe/dichromate feature is claimed.

13. **Calculation UI.** General mode presents actual supplied amounts, audited scope, charge contributions, optional explicit recipe doses, derived pH/Eh, residual carriers and generated source-redox reactions where the decomposition is defined. Incompatible or supersaturated candidates report why no accepted result exists. Ordinary/imposed modes and the reviewed experiment remain separate. Editing amounts removes stale answers. Live browser verification confirmed a peroxide general calculation, derived pH/Eh, stale-result removal, missing-countercharge refusal, recipe addition, and reviewed-to-general Fe routing. The returning-mode path was corrected and rechecked after creating a fresh setup.

14. **Wet Lab API readiness.** `calculations/closedChemistry.js` exposes `prepareReagent`, `mixPreparations`, `auditClosedNetwork`, `prepareClosedEquilibrium` and `solveClosedEquilibrium`. A caller mixes source-bound preparations, supplies the resulting analytical amounts and selected identities to discovery/preparation, and receives either accepted conditional chemistry or typed refusal. No UI component owns a second chemistry engine. This is a molal, explicit-solvent-mass contract; Wet Lab must not reinterpret it as volume mixing or add undisclosed counterions. A Wet Lab UI was not built.

15. **Regression and preservation evidence.** The initial baseline passed 594/594. The focused scientific/recipe/UI-adapter suite passed 43/43. The final full regression passed 605/605 with zero failures, cancellations, skips or todo (515398.3874 ms), including the return-mode and capacity failure controls. The final label-collision safeguard was tightened after that run started; the affected-path suite passed 43/43 afterward. See `open-closed-regression.txt` and `open-closed-focused.txt` for the exact evidence. Production build and artifact audit pass; lint has no errors and one pre-existing ExpandedPlot effect-cleanup warning. The browser evidence is recorded in `open-closed-browser.txt`. `open-closed-hashes.json` records before/after file hashes and explicit changes. Imported source data/constants, original Step-3/4/5 evidence, reviewed Fe/peroxide metadata/backend/sweep, point-solver equations/tolerances, reference fixtures and golden artifacts are preserved. A legacy general-discovery test was intentionally updated from expecting the old Fe capacity refusal to requiring full chloride-redox inclusion; the reviewed chemistry expectations were not changed.

16. **Remaining limitations.** Only 12 source component identities have reviewed seed allocation; successful propagation does not establish arbitrary oxidation-state metadata. Every network still needs complete source closure. The path is ideal at 25 °C and declared 1 bar, aqueous-only with explicit phase/gas diagnostics, finite resource bounds, no general closed addition sweep, no finite headspace, no stock density/volume conversion and no kinetics. Neutral recipes improve preparation but do not make excluded precipitation disappear. General usability has expanded without silently turning incomplete chemistry into an accepted result.
